Free Guide, 2026

Your Guide to Christmas 2026

Festive snacks, the Christmas parade, where to find Santa, New Year's Eve, and for the first time ever, Christmas in Arendelle. This is my first Christmas at Disneyland Paris too, so we're doing this together.

🎄

Main Street & the Tree

The holiday entrance is back with giant baubles, Mickey wishing you happy holidays, and Christmas topiary on either side shaped into hearts and snowflakes. Above it, the Disneyland Railroad station is draped in garlands and baubles, and even the lampposts and Main Street vehicles have been dressed up. There isn't a shop on Main Street that isn't covered in tinsel, garlands and lights.

And the big news: the Main Street garlands are back. After none at Halloween I was fiercely worried they were gone for good, but they've returned, and this time they're wrapped in ribbon.

The Town Square tree is 78ft tall and lights up several times throughout the day, so don't plan your whole day around it. You'll catch it at some point.

📸
Photo spot: step up onto the little podium by the gazebo in Town Square. The garlands frame the castle perfectly from there.

Speaking of the castle, the walls have come down after six months and the bridge is open again, so you can get your perfect castle shots this Christmas. Central Plaza has been decorated too, with snowflake topiary and the lights around it swapped for coloured snowflake bulbs.

🍪

Festive Food & Drink

Disneyland Paris is notorious for having the worst park food of all the Disney parks, but the Christmas menus are out, so I'm taking one for the team to find out what's on the nice list and what belongs on the naughty list. Here's where to find it all.

  • Boardwalk Candy Palace: a red velvet cupcake, a vanilla Mickey cupcake topped with a Christmas tree, a chocolate cake pop and the Mickey cookie.
  • Christmas market chalets: in Fantasyland, and in Disney Adventure World next to Remy's Place.
  • Cowboy Cookout and Café Hyperion: a new Christmas burger. It's the same burger in both, so you don't need to try it twice.
  • Lucky Nugget Saloon: a gingerbread bun burger for the holiday season.
  • Au Chalet de la Marionnette: a half chicken Christmas special, plus a festive coffee break with two kinds of coffee and a hot chocolate.
  • Disney Village: a new Christmas drinks menu. I've tried the Christmas cocktail and it was delicious.
  • Nordic Crowns Tavern: a Christmas menu over in World of Frozen. More on that in the Arendelle section below.
🍽️
I'm trying everything. My full Christmas snack review is coming soon, so subscribe on YouTube so you don't miss it.
🛍️

Christmas Merch

Christmas is the busiest time of year at Disneyland Paris and the shops really go all out. I've got a whole video on the 2026 Christmas merch, so head to my YouTube channel to see every piece. For the Frozen Christmas merch, skip ahead to the Arendelle section.

🚧

Opening Hours & Ride Closures

Yes, both parks are open on Christmas Day. Hours vary across the season, so always check the Disneyland Paris app ahead of time.

  • Crush's Coaster: closed for an extended refurbishment, back in summer 2027.
  • Disneyland Railroad, Main Street Station: closed November 2 to 7. You can still ride from Fantasyland Station.
  • Les Voyages de Pinocchio: closed November 2 to 13, reopening November 14.
  • Frontierland Playground: closed November 12 to 16.
  • Phantom Manor: closed November 16 to 27.
  • Pirate Galleon: remains closed for ongoing maintenance.
🎅

Character Meets

  • Santa has a new address this year: Disney Clothiers on Main Street. From the set-up at the back of the shop (Santa's coat, Mrs. Claus's cloak, and milk and cookies), my guess is you'll queue there and exit into the shop. It hasn't been confirmed yet, but given how small the space is, I'd expect a virtual queue rather than standby.
  • Stitch is meeting at Videopolis in Discoveryland. A cast member told me they expect it to be standby, not a virtual queue. He's very popular, so if you have Extra Magic Hours, use them for Stitch.
  • Jack Skellington is sticking around after Halloween at the Frontierland Theatre, just behind the purple curtains. Sadly no Sally this time.
⚠️
Jack is a virtual queue, and it's competitive. If Halloween is anything to go by, be on the app five minutes before it opens, keep refreshing, and hit select the second it goes live. Those slots fill up fast.

Your backup: festive gatherings

If you miss out on a virtual queue, these are your safety net.

  • 13:00: Mickey and friends in Town Square.
  • 13:15: the Disney Princesses on Central Plaza.
  • 14:25: Princess Aurora and Prince Phillip in the Castle Courtyard.

Over in World of Frozen you'll find Anna, Elsa and Olaf, and Kristoff is free roaming for the first time. Characters aren't guaranteed and can depend on weather, so always check the app on the day.

✨

Mickey's Dazzling Christmas Parade

The biggest thing on the calendar runs twice a day: once in the daytime and once after sunset, so you get the full effect of the lights. It's included with your park ticket.

November parade times

  • 7 to 16 November: 15:55 and 17:45
  • 17 November: 14:20 and 16:00
  • 18 November: 15:55 and 17:45
  • 19 November: 14:20 and 16:00
  • 20 to 25 November: 15:55 and 17:45
  • 26 November: 14:20 and 16:00
  • 27 to 30 November: 15:55 and 17:45

December times haven't been announced yet. All times are subject to change, so check the app on the day.

Where to stand

Daytime parade: if you have little ones, aim for the railings around Central Plaza. It doesn't really matter which one, because the characters move between the stages, so you'll see everyone.

After-sunset parade: stand on Main Street outside Flora's, the Emporium or the train station, where the street juts out. Check there isn't a tree between you and the Christmas tree, because you want a clear view of it lit up while the parade goes past.

Reserved viewing from €21

Disney now sells reserved viewing for the parade. I haven't tried it, so I can't say whether it's worth it, but for a family of five you're over €100 for one parade. Personally I'd spend that elsewhere and just get there early.

🎶

Christmas Shows

  • Minnie Mouse and Mrs. Claus star in a brand new show on the Castle Stage. Details are still under wraps and times aren't out yet, so keep an eye on the app. There's an advent calendar in the shape of the castle on the stage, with windows from 1 to 25 December, so I'm wondering if a window gets opened each day.
  • Let's Sing Christmas! at Videopolis Theatre, with Mickey, Minnie and friends singing all the Christmas classics. Perfect if you love a sing-song.
❄️

Christmas in Arendelle

For the first time ever, Christmas has arrived in World of Frozen at Disney Adventure World, and this is the one not to miss. Christmas isn't just a Disneyland Park thing anymore, so plan to split your time between both parks.

As you walk in, there's a big Christmas tree decorated with Frozen in mind. Look closely and you'll spot a gingerbread Kristoff, Sven, Queen Elsa and Queen Anna. The whole village is decked out, with snowflake and mountain goat banners and garlands full of berries and ribbon along the shop façades. It feels very Scandi.

Two new shows

  • At the foot of North Mountain: about 15 minutes, with songs from Olaf's Frozen Adventure, Queen Elsa, Queen Anna, Olaf and the Arendelle Carollers. I've heard a rumour that Elsa might make it snow over the village, so here's hoping.
  • On Arendelle Bay: about seven minutes, with Anna and Elsa on the water in brand new winter outfits. It runs several times a day, including late afternoon.

Characters

Keep an eye out as you wander, because some characters are free roaming: Oaken, Mossie and, for the first time ever, Kristoff. Meets aren't guaranteed and can depend on the weather.

Food & merch

Nordic Crowns Tavern has a Christmas menu of sliced chicken with cranberry sauce, butternut squash, chestnuts and crushed potatoes. It wasn't available when I visited, so I'll be trying it in a future video. Arendelle Boutique is getting new Scandi-inspired Christmas merch, and it was so popular there was a queue just to get into the shop.

⛵

Beating the Frozen Ever After Queue

Frozen Ever After is going to be hectic over the holidays, so you need a ride strategy. Here are your options.

  • Single rider: great if your party doesn't mind splitting up.
  • Go during the parade: when Mickey's Dazzling Christmas Parade is on in Disneyland Park, a lot of people head over there, so the wait here should drop.
  • Premier Access: if it's an absolute must do, buy it and you're guaranteed your ride.
  • Join the queue just before closing: my favourite. As long as you're in the queue before the park closes, you'll get to ride, and by then most people are watching the fireworks or the drone show, so it's practically a walk on.
🎆

New Year's Eve

You've got a decision to make this year. Disney Adventure World usually closes around 22:00 on New Year's Eve and everyone celebrates in Disneyland Park, but this year both parks are open for the countdown, closing around 01:00 for now.

  • Disneyland Park: DJ parties hosted by Disney characters, the countdown projected onto the castle, then fireworks.
  • Disney Adventure World: a drone show followed by fireworks. Rumour has it the drone show is heavily inspired by Frozen.
🌧️
The drones depend on the weather. If it's a stormy night they won't go up, so factor that in when you choose where to ring in the new year.

My top tip

Christmas now spans both parks, so don't spend your whole trip on Main Street. Save your evenings for Arendelle, and use the parade as your window to ride Frozen Ever After while everyone else is watching.
